# Module: Law, zoning and housing policy (`src/visionhousing/law/`)

**What it does.** Keeps the rules that bear on the five benchmark reforms at three levels: the state (the planning code and the bills of the current session), the county (who zones where, and the county plan) and the municipality (the ADU rule, the smallest minimum lot, parking minimums, the housing types allowed, transportation impact fees). They go into four small tables of the state pack, and the place profile shows them with their citations.

**Why.** A state reform changes different things in different places. HB 2186 changes little where ADUs are already allowed by right, and nothing in Philadelphia or Pittsburgh if a bill only amends the Municipalities Planning Code. People need to see, for their place, which rules apply today, which bill would change them, and how sure we are.

## Terms

- **MPC**: the Pennsylvania Municipalities Planning Code (Act 247 of 1968). It lets municipalities plan and zone. It does not reach Philadelphia (a first-class city) or Pittsburgh (a second-class city), which zone under other laws.
- **By right**: allowed without a hearing, if the written standards are met. **Conditional use**: decided by the governing body after a hearing. **Special exception**: decided by the zoning hearing board after a hearing.
- **Not addressed**: someone read the ordinance and it does not list ADUs as a use. That alone is **not** "prohibited". What an unlisted use may do is set by the ordinance's own clause on unlisted uses (`unlisted_uses`): `prohibited` only where a verified exclusivity clause says uses not listed are not allowed; `conditional` or `special_exception` where unlisted uses may ask for a hearing; empty when that clause was not read, and then the ADU answer is unknown. **Unknown**: nobody has read it, or the reading does not answer the question. Unknown is never shown as "no".
- **Verified**: a person found the quote at the stated offset in the municipality's own text, on the stated date. **Draft**: anything less, including everything the rule-based reader produces.
- **Scope** of a provision: `all`, `all_zoned`, `mpc` (all but Philadelphia and Pittsburgh), `mpc_zoned`, or `conditional` (a test the tool cannot apply, such as HB 1459's county-growth test).

## How each level is built

**State.** `law/state.py` downloads the MPC's full text from palegis.us once (the site's robots file allows it), strips the HTML and keeps the SHA-256 of the text with white space collapsed, so the pack names exactly the text that was read. Every provision in `state_provisions.yaml` cites a section; the build fails if that section's heading (`Section 503-A.`) is not in the text. The summaries are our own words. Each bill's page gives its last action and date; a bill the page shows as signed is `enacted`, one still open after the session's last day (`session_end`, November 30, 2026) is `lapsed`, otherwise `pending`. If a page cannot be read, the hand-checked values in the YAML are used and the build notes say so.

Provisions recorded (read in the fetched text on 2026-11-23): the MPC has no ADU section (left to each zoning ordinance, § 603); § 604(4) housing of all basic dwelling types; § 301(a)(2.1) the housing plan; § 605(3) density bonuses; Article VII-A traditional neighbourhood development; § 605(2)(i) special rules near transit terminals; § 604(1) and § 301(a)(3) parking; Article V-A §§ 503-A, 503-A(d), 504-A transportation impact fees (the only impact fee the MPC authorizes, and not for counties); § 507-A tap-in fees; § 503(1) review fees. Bills: HB 2186 and SB 1346 (ADUs), HB 2155 (no parking minimums), HB 1459 (apartments by right in commercial zones with at most one space per home). Only bills are listed; co-sponsorship memos are not.

The Pennsylvania Code and Bulletin site disallows all automated reading in its robots file. It is a `link_only` document: listed, never fetched.

**County.** `county_land_use` counts, from the DCED record, the municipalities with no zoning of their own where the county's ordinance applies (MPC § 602) and their residents, and those with no zoning at all. The county plan and the planning commission's link come from `assumptions/plans.yaml`, now for all 67 counties. Every link was opened on the date given; a plan whose page gives no adoption year, or a page that could not be opened, is `verified: false` and the profile says "not confirmed". Five counties have no current plan found online (`name: null`).

**Municipality.** Rules come only from texts the municipality publishes itself (its own website or its own legislative system) or, where the county zones for the municipality (MPC § 602), from the county's ordinance on the county's own website. Commercial code hosts (eCode360, Municode, American Legal) forbid bulk or automated use; we keep no data from them and the reader refuses their addresses. On 2026-11-23 these were read by hand: Pittsburgh (ADUs by right citywide, Ordinance 31 of 2026), Shrewsbury Borough, Waynesboro Borough, Wyomissing Borough, Derry Township (Dauphin), the City of Hermitage, the City of Warren (ADUs permitted in its four residential and mixed-use districts, Ordinance 1978 of 2024), and two county ordinances: Fayette County's (2006, with its 2009 use table), which applies in 31 of the county's 42 municipalities, and Warren County's (amended February 2026), which names the 14 municipalities it covers. Neither county ordinance mentions ADUs, so those 45 municipalities are `not_addressed`. All of these are verified. Philadelphia stays a draft: a 2022 city checklist, not the code; the city's zoning legislation table (updated November 6, 2026) shows the only pending ADU bill, 250042, as introduced, so no ADU bill was enacted in 2026. A county ordinance gives one row per municipality it covers. Which ones it covers comes from the county's own text or zoning page, because the DCED record can differ (for Warren County it lists five townships the ordinance does not name, and misses two it does). In some counties the DCED record says "county" where the county only has topic ordinances (solar, wind, towers) or airport zoning, not general zoning (Somerset, Potter, Clarion); those were not counted. The 2026-11 calibration file `adu_legal_share.yaml` cites 221 town readings, but only its totals are in this repository; its per-town notes are not, so they could not become rows. On 2026-11-24, following the review-priority list, 32 more were read from their own sites the same way: in Allegheny County Penn Hills, Monroeville, Upper St. Clair, Hampton, McKeesport, Robinson, Pine, Whitehall, West Deer, O'Hara, Swissvale, Findlay, Ohio, Duquesne, Tarentum, North Braddock, Pitcairn, East McKeesport and East Pittsburgh; elsewhere Millcreek (Erie), Fox (Elk), Allentown, Bethlehem's Lehigh part and Upper Milford (Lehigh), Dunmore (Lackawanna), Frankstown (Blair), Guilford (Franklin), Neshannock (Lawrence), Lower Paxton (Dauphin), and Dingman, Delaware and Milford townships (Pike). On 2026-11-26, 23 more were added, every quote found again by script at its offset in the saved copy whose text SHA-256 is the document's: York city and York township (York); Upper Paxton, Highspire, Millersburg, Conewago and Royalton (Dauphin); and the Lycoming County ordinance, one row for each of the 16 municipalities that both its own text (Division 1400.A and its per-municipality zoning maps) and the DCED record put under county zoning. ## Coverage three ways: residents, lots, land

Counting municipalities says little: one city holds more homes than thirty townships. And the ADU benchmark acts on lots, not people. So coverage is measured three ways (`law/coverage.py`), from tables the pack already has; nothing new is downloaded.

- **Single-family lots** (`sf_lots`). The ACS count of one-unit detached homes in each tract (`tracts.units_1_detached`) stands for the lots the benchmark could reach. A tract that spans several municipalities is split among them in proportion to their 2020 housing units in that tract (`tract_municipalities`). One source, the same way, in every county; the county parcel layers cover only Philadelphia and Allegheny, so they are not used here.
- **Residential land** (`res_land_ha`). Each census block with homes (`blocks`) counts its land shared among its homes, but at most one acre per home (`land_weight.max_m2_per_home` in `assumptions/law/hooks.yaml`), so a large rural block with one farmhouse counts one acre, not its whole area. Blocks with no homes count nothing. Split among municipalities like the lots. It is a proxy for the land homes sit on, not a parcel map; it is reported, never used in an estimate.
- **Residents** (2024 estimates), as before.

`county_coverage` gives, per county, the share of residents, lots and land under a verified reading that is in force on the run's as-of date (the hooks' own test, `law/inforce.py`); `county_land_use` stores the counts behind them. The engine's `LocalLaw.coverage` has residents and lots (land is empty there, because the municipal table does not carry it). **The switch rule is unchanged**: a county uses its own ADU and parking values only when verified rows cover at least 50% of its *residents*. The lot share is printed beside it in every explanation (the ledger note and the county panel's plain words), so a reader sees when the two differ: in Allegheny, Pittsburgh is 25% of residents but 19% of single-family lots.

**Coverage after the 2026-11-26 readings** (verified rows in force on the as-of date, as `visionhousing law-tables` and `county_land_use` report them; statewide 12.5% of residents and 12.8% of single-family lots, up from 11.7% and 12.1%):

| County | Residents read | Single-family lots read | Residential land read | 50% residents bar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dauphin | 44.8% to 49.0% | 34.6% to 39.9% | 33.4% to 39.4% | not met (about 2,800 residents short) |
| York | 0.8% to 17.1% | 1.1% to 10.7% | 1.0% to 9.8% | not met |
| Lycoming | 0% to 10.4% | 0% to 14.9% | 0% to 17.8% | not met |

No county crossed the bar, so the counties that use their own ADU and parking values are unchanged (Fayette, Pike and Warren for parking; none for the ADU hook) and every benchmark headline is unchanged. The lot-weighted ADU categories and the "What this law changes" view do change: statewide, the ADU benchmark's unresolved share of single-family lots falls from 90.4% to 89.9% and its changed-condition share rises from 6.3% to 6.9%.

The weights are added by `visionhousing law-tables`, which reads the built pack. A full `make data` builds the law tables before the pack's tract tables exist, so run `law-tables` after it; without the weights, coverage is by residents only and the lot columns are empty.

## ADU legal-status categories and the benchmark's exposure

Each municipality's rule falls into one of five categories (`adu_category`; the mapping is in `hooks.yaml` under `adu_categories`):

| Category | Rows | What the ADU benchmark does there |
|---|---|---|
| `already_permitted` | by right, a detached ADU allowed, no owner-occupancy rule, no occupancy restriction | nothing: already equivalent |
| `by_right_conditions` | by right, but attached only, or an owner rule, or for relatives only (`occupancy_restriction: relatives_only`), or conditions not read | changes the status quo (drops conditions) |
| `discretionary` | conditional use or special exception | changes it (no hearing) |
| `prohibited` | expressly prohibited, or not provided for as a use (`not_addressed`) where a verified exclusivity clause bars unlisted uses | changes it (newly allowed) |
| `unknown` | not read, read but not verified, not yet in effect, or `not_addressed` with no clause on unlisted uses read | unknown |

**Relatives-only units.** The benchmark ADU may be lived in by any household. A by-right unit only for relatives (Bethlehem's and Lebanon's "unit for care of a relative", Palmer's relative-care ADU, Buckingham's in-law dwelling, Pine's family ADU) does not meet it, so `occupancy_restrictions` in `hooks.yaml` sends it to `by_right_conditions` whatever its other flags say. There, as for every by-right-with-conditions lot, the ADU estimate keeps the ledger share `adu.conditions_exposure_share` of its lots exposed to the reform. The resident-weighted `a + b x s` rule (used only where no lot-weighted categories exist) still counts it in `a`, like any by-right row.

**Not addressed is not prohibited** (changed 2026-11-25). A `not_addressed` row goes to `prohibited` only with `unlisted_uses: prohibited` (a verified exclusivity clause, cited in `unlisted_uses_cite`), to `discretionary` with `conditional` or `special_exception` (`not_addressed_by_unlisted_uses` in `hooks.yaml`), and otherwise to `unknown`, which the ADU hook counts as unread (`b`), never as "not legal". Of the 124 not-addressed rows, 9 have an exclusivity clause (Frankstown, Penn Hills, Upper St. Clair, O'Hara, Findlay, Ohio, North Braddock, Pitcairn, East Pittsburgh), 59 a hearing route (54 special exception: Shrewsbury, McKeesport, Dingman and the Schuylkill and Luzerne county ordinances; 5 conditional use: Hampton, Tarentum, East McKeesport, Clairton, Palmyra), and 56 no clause read (the Fayette and Warren county ordinances and 11 municipalities): those are now unknown.

**Interface for the engine (E2).** `local_law_for_engine(rules, munis)` returns a `LocalLaw` whose new fields are filled for every county, whether or not it passes the 50% bar:

- `LocalLaw.categories`: one row per county (`county_adu_categories`), columns `county`, `sf_lots`, `residents`, `lots_<category>` and `share_lots_<category>` and `share_residents_<category>` for the five categories, `lots_changed` (by_right_conditions + discretionary + prohibited: lots where the benchmark changes the status quo), `lots_equivalent` (already_permitted), `share_lots_changed_of_read` (changed / (changed + equivalent); empty when nothing is read) and `weight` (`sf_lots`, or `residents` when the rules table has no lot weights, in which case the `lots_` columns hold residents).
- `LocalLaw.adu_categories(county)`: category to share of the county's lots.
- `LocalLaw.coverage` and `LocalLaw.lot_coverage(county)`: coverage three ways.

The existing coefficients (`share = a + b x statewide share`) are unchanged. E2 may use the categories to split the funnel's first step, for example to apply a separate assumption to discretionary lots; the categories never create a value for unknown lots.

## Rule-change tracker

Every provision, bill and municipal rule carries its dates and limits:

| Field | State code section | Bill | Municipal rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| `introduced_on` | — | first dated action on the bill page (the referral, which happens the day a bill is introduced) | — |
| `enacted_on` | the date the compiler's note says the section (or its article) was added; else the act's date, July 31, 1968 | date of an action saying the Governor approved it | — |
| `last_amended` | the latest compiler's note in the section | — | `amended_through` of the document |
| `adopted_on` | — | — | the document's adoption date |
| `in_force_by` | — | — | where no adoption date is printed: the latest dated enactment inside the cited codified section (for example `[Added 6-1-15 by Ord. No. 2136]`), or the posted text's "enacted to" date. The section as read stood in force by then; it is not an adoption date |
| `effective_date` | January 1, 1969 (the code's § 102) for original sections; unknown for later additions (their notes do not give it) | from the bill text, when read | the document's, when stated |
| `binding_conditions` | — | plain words (e.g. HB 2155: one year after enactment) | plain words (e.g. Hermitage: owner lives on the lot) |
| verification | `verification`: heading found in the fetched text | `verification`: page read at build time, or `checked_values` | `status`: verified or draft |
| last checked | `checked_on` | `checked_on` | `reviewed_on` |

Dates may be a year or a month when that is all the source says; nothing is guessed. `future_effective` is 1 when a full effective date is after the build date. **Adoption, publication and effective dates are kept apart, and adoption never stands in for effectiveness** (changed 2026-11-25). **A rule counts as read in the hooks only once it is in force on the run's as-of date** (`as_of` in `assumptions/outcome.yaml`, or the `as_of` passed to `local_law_for_engine`), not the build date: a known `effective_date` must be on or before it (a year or year-month counts from its last possible day), and a known effective date always decides, so a future one keeps the rule unread whatever else is known; with no effective date, a known `in_force_by` on or before it is enough (Upper St. Clair, Whitehall in Allegheny, Findlay, Milford Township in Pike and Buckingham carry one); with neither, the rule counts from its `reviewed_on` date, the first day a person saw the text posted as the municipality's law, and never earlier (`unknown_effective: read_from_reviewed_on` in `hooks.yaml`, the same rule as a timeline record with `start_unknown`); an `adopted_on` after the as-of date still rules it out. An adoption date alone never puts a rule in force. On the run's as-of date (2026-11-24) this changes one reading: Derry Township (Dauphin), which has no date at all, now counts from its reading on 2026-11-23. Unread residents keep the statewide range. **The coverage tables and the ADU categories use the same test** (`law/inforce.py`, one helper called by both `law/hooks.py` and `law/coverage.py`), so the residents and lots counted as read in `county_land_use`, `LocalLaw.coverage` and the categories always match what the hooks use; a verified row with a blank or `unknown` `adu_status` is `unknown` in the categories, as it is unread for the ADU hook. The build-time `future_effective` flag stays in the table for readers but no count uses it. The whole code was re-enacted in 1988; the notes record later changes only, so "1968" means "in the code since the original act or its 1988 re-enactment".

## Implementation timeline (`law/timeline.py`)

A law does not switch on in one step. The timeline keeps each step as a record: **one status of one provision, for one jurisdiction (a state, county or municipality code) or one mapped area inside it (a GeoJSON polygon), for one housing type, over one period** (`start` to `end`). Records live in `assumptions/law/timeline.yaml`.

- Statuses: `pending` (a bill), `enacted` (signed, not yet known to be in effect), `legally_effective`, `stayed` (paused by a court), `locally_implemented` (written into local rules or guidance), `practically_usable` (permits can actually be had). Each record also has `court_status`, `enforcement_status`, `scope`, `exclusions`, the `agency`, `implementation_evidence`, `text_version`, `retrieved_on`, its sources and, optionally, `source_id` (the source-registry entry it is shown under in the place profile; without it the closest registry address is used).
- **Supersession.** A replaced rule ends on the date its replacement takes effect (`end`, `superseded_by`); the new record names what it replaces (`supersedes`).
- **Start unknown.** When a record's own source does not state when it took effect, `start` stays empty and the record says `start_unknown: true` (a verified record with no start must say so). It then counts only **on or after its `verified_on` date**, the first day a person saw it in force; before that it is not taken as in force, so no rule is made to look older than the evidence shows. Harrisburg's ADU rule and Lackawaxen's accessory apartment rule count from November 24, 2026. The two Vermont "before the Act" records end on July 1, 2026, before they were verified, so they apply on no date; they are kept for the supersession link.
- **Two kinds of date.** `start`/`end` say when the status held in law ("took effect"); `verified_on`/`end_verified_on` say when a person confirmed it. A run made as of an earlier date (`as_of`) sees only records verified by then and ignores an end verified later, so an older run reproduces exactly. An `end` with no `end_verified_on` is a **scheduled sunset** written in the provision itself: it was known when the record was verified, so a run as of a date before the sunset still sees the rule end on it.
- **Review queue.** `bill_review_queue` compares each bill's page with its latest verified record; a new action or a signature becomes a `review: unverified` record. Unverified records change nothing until a person checks the authoritative text and marks them verified.

**The engine's call.** `timeline.eligibility(state, housing_type, on, geoid=..., point=..., as_of=..., state_fips=...)` (the state's two-digit code is filled in from its postal code when not given, so records written as `jurisdiction: "42"` apply) returns the status that applies, whether the rule is `in_force` (True only for legally effective, locally implemented or practically usable, and not stayed; False for pending, enacted-not-effective and stayed; None when no record applies, which means unknown, never "no"), the record used, its level (area, municipality, county, state), its verification date and sources. The most specific record wins; among equals, the latest `start`. A `start_unknown` record ranks below every dated record at its level: its `verified_on` only decides from when it counts, so it never outranks a newer dated amendment. An area record applies only to a point inside its polygon.

The run contract (`engine/outcome.py`) reads `effective_date` and `status` from `state_law_provisions`; the tracker columns use the same name.

## Provision versions (`law_provision_version`)

Each timeline record is one version of one operative provision, so the `law_provision_version` table is a view of the records, not a second store: `timeline.provision_versions(records)` returns one row per record with `jurisdiction`, `citation`, `section`, `source_url`, `version_state` (`final` enacted text or `draft`), `effective_start` / `effective_end` (with `effective_start_unknown`), `adopted_on`, `published_on`, `verified_on`, `last_checked_on`, `supersedes` / `superseded_by`, `court_status` (a stay), `higher_law_floor` (the state rule local law cannot go below), `source_fingerprint` (`sha256:` of the text read, and what was hashed) and `excerpt_locator` (where the operative words are). A draft text can never be in force (the record is refused). The table is built from the YAML files and is a pack table in every state: `law_tables.provision_version_table(state)` keeps the records whose jurisdiction is the state's code or a county or municipality code starting with its FIPS code, and `law_tables.add_provision_versions(folder, cache)` writes `law_provision_version.csv.gz` and rewrites the data dictionary and manifest. A full build runs it as the add-on step "law provision versions" (`RETROFITS` in `data/pack.py`); `visionhousing law-tables --state XX` refreshes it in any state (and, where the pack has `municipal_planning`, the four municipal and state tables too). A state with no verified provision (Massachusetts, Wyoming on 2026-11-25) gets an empty table with its columns.

## State floors by date and the current-law convention (`law/rights.py`)

`state_floor(district, on)` says what Vermont's § 4412 guarantees in one district on one date. The version comes from the timeline record in force that day; the terms (two units for the duplex right, four for the small-multiunit right, the service conditions, the 2028 exception's threshold, the ADU's yield to flood hazard and fluvial erosion bylaws under § 4424) come from `assumptions/law/statutory_rights.yaml`. A district is described by what is known about it (year-round residential, residential allowed, municipal water, municipal sewer, the number of units it requires in multiunit buildings, its own by-right maximum, a § 4424 bylaw); anything not known is `None`, and an unknown input that could matter gives an unknown answer, never "no right".

- **Scope test of the 2028 change.** The exception removes the four-unit right only where the district is known to require more than four units; where that is not known the answer is unknown.
- **Count only the change.** `legal_increment(current, proposed)` lists the unit counts a proposal newly allows: a served district already allowing four units, with a proposal allowing six, gains five and six; equal rights give a pure legalization increment of zero. Where the empirical baseline already reflects earlier rights, only the future change in expected authorizations relative to those rights should be added, so ongoing production is not counted twice.
- **Current law in both arms.** An enacted change with a later effective date (the 2028 text) applies from its date in the baseline and in the reform arm alike; a pending bill never does. "Freeze today's rules" is a separately named comparison (`freeze_comparison` in `statutory_rights.yaml`, `freeze_on` 2026-11-25): `freeze_difference(district, dates)` and `timeline.schedule_comparison(...)` show both, date by date, and mark where they differ (from January 1, 2028, in a served district that requires more than four units). The profile's state-law fact names a scheduled change and this comparison.
- **Missing middle uses the schedule.** `middle_floor_schedule(state, years)` reads the block `missing_middle_application` of `statutory_rights.yaml` (states listed: Vermont) and gives, for lots with municipal water and sewer, without, and with unknown service, the units already allowed by right in each window year (read on July 1), under current law and frozen, and the rights the permit history already reflects (Act 47's duplex and four-unit rights since July 1, 2023). Its `comparator` is the larger of that year's rights and the history's, so the funnel never counts production the baseline already holds. The reform's lots are in districts whose own bylaw allows one home, which require no multiunit buildings, so the 2028 exception does not reach them. The engine hands this to the missing-middle funnel (`engine/law_floor.py`); see the [missing-middle method](../methods/missing_middle.md#a-state-floor-by-date-vermont).

Records verified on 2026-11-25 are seen by runs whose as-of date is on or after that day; the shipped as-of date (2026-11-24) reproduces the earlier run.

## Massachusetts ADU rights and what the benchmark adds (`law/ma_adu.py`)

Since February 2, 2025, M.G.L. c. 40A § 3 protects one ADU by right in a single-family residential district. The terms are parameters in `assumptions/law/statutory_rights.yaml` (provision `ma_40a_3_adu`, version `ma_adu_by_right_effective`); the dates are the timeline records in `assumptions/law/states/ma.yaml`. The statute text was read by script on malegislature.gov (robots file checked first; § 1A, § 3 and St. 2024 c. 150 re-read on 2026-11-26, unchanged).

- **Size (§ 1A).** The protected ADU may be capped at the smaller of half the principal dwelling's gross floor area and 900 sq ft (`protected_size_sqft`). A 1,000 sq ft house gets 500 sq ft; a 1,600 sq ft house gets 800.
- **Parking (§ 3).** A town may require at most one added space, and none for an ADU within 0.5 miles of a commuter rail station, subway station, ferry terminal or bus station.
- **Owner occupancy.** May not be required, so the benchmark adds nothing here.
- **Scope.** Single-family districts of the cities and towns that zone under chapter 40A. Boston zones under its own act (St. 1956 c. 665), so the right does not reach it. What was read: § 3's other paragraphs name Boston where they reach it ("including ... the city of Boston"); the ADU paragraph does not; St. 2024 c. 150 §§ 7-8 amend chapter 40A only. The 1956 act itself is not on malegislature.gov, so its text was not read.

`ma_adu_right(lot, on)` gives the state floor for one lot on one date; `benchmark_gain(lot, on, min_size_sqft=600)` says what the benchmark (600 sq ft, detached or semi-detached, no added parking) adds: `full` where the state gives no by-right ADU of that size (Boston, outside a single-family district, the size cap below 600 and the town allows no more, or no version in force), `conditions` where the only gap is the one parking space, `none` where it adds nothing. Unknown inputs give an unknown answer, never "no right".

**In a run (named variant `ma_adu_state_floor`, option `legal: ma_state_floor`).** The benchmark keeps its labelled statewide already-legal range (`assumptions/calibration/already_legal_ma.yaml`). The variant replaces that range (it never adds to it, so nothing is counted twice) with each tract's share worked out from the lot test: `legal = (1 - Boston share) x single-family district share x size share x (1 - homes beyond 0.5 mi x towns requiring the space x adu.conditions_exposure_share)`, with `size share = (1 - houses under 1,200 sq ft) + houses under 1,200 sq ft x towns already allowing 600 sq ft`. From the pack: each tract's share of 2020 homes within 0.5 mile (straight line) of a commuter rail, subway or ferry stop (GTFS route types 2; 0 and 1; 4). A bus station cannot be told apart from a bus stop, so none counts: the parking gap is an upper bound. Boston's share of each tract comes from `tract_municipalities` (county 25025, county subdivision 07000), added to the Massachusetts pack on 2026-11-26 by the tract-crosswalk step (Boston: 207 whole tracts). A pack without that table falls back to the judged share `adu.ma_boston_share_of_suffolk_lots` for every Suffolk tract, and a run note says which was used. Measured instead of judged (seed 0, 2,000 draws): the variant moves from 4,269 (1,710 to 9,043) to 4,292 (1,721 to 9,078) added ADUs over five years, the paired change against the benchmark from +1,846 (+632 to +4,227) to +1,873 (+645 to +4,287), above the benchmark in 100% of draws either way, and the state-floor already-legal share from 0.45 (0.37 to 0.52) to 0.44 (0.37 to 0.52). The benchmark (2,365, 914 to 4,977) is unchanged. The shares the pack cannot measure are labelled judgement ranges in the ledger: `adu.ma_sf_district_share`, `adu.ma_primary_below_floor_share` (defined at 1,200 sq ft; the code refuses a benchmark size that would change that line), `adu.ma_local_size_allows_benchmark_share` and `adu.ma_town_requires_space_share`. The run's record `already_legal_share_state_floor` is the lot-weighted share in each draw. The variant sits in the ADU Alternatives table for Massachusetts.

## Place weights for a bill that leaves a city out (`law/places.py`)

SB 1346 leaves Pittsburgh out, but Pittsburgh sits inside Allegheny County. `place_tract_shares(tract_municipalities, "42003", "61000")` gives each Allegheny tract's share of 2020 housing units inside the city, from the Census block assignment file already in the pack; `block_place_shares(blocks, boundary)` does the same by putting each block's internal point inside or outside a boundary polygon; `county_place_share` weighs the tract shares by single-family detached homes. Run on the PA pack on 2026-11-25: Pittsburgh's 2020 tracts nest exactly in the city (128 of Allegheny's 394 tracts, none split; 157,695 housing units), and the block points inside the Census TIGER/Line 2020 county subdivision outline give the same tract shares. Pittsburgh holds 70,572 of Allegheny's 373,731 single-family detached homes, **18.9%**, inside the flat stub's 15% to 25% range (central 20%). The City's own current boundary (WPRDC "Pittsburgh City Boundary", licence not stated, used only as a check) gives 19.1% (one tract partly inside). Since 2026-11-25 (L1-R1) the SB 1346 run uses these per-tract shares: the engine (`engine/place_scope.py`, which reads the law layer so the scenario module does not) passes each Allegheny tract's measured share to the ADU funnel, which keeps `1 - share` of the tract's lots. Pittsburgh's own tracts are left out and the suburbs keep all their lots, instead of every Allegheny tract losing a drawn 15% to 25%. The county-wide share is the run's record `place_pittsburgh_share`. The flat ledger share `adu.pittsburgh_share_of_allegheny_lots` is kept only as the fallback for a pack without `tract_municipalities`, and a run note says when it is used.

## Vermont and Wyoming local readings (`law/local_readings.py`)

Vermont and Wyoming packs have no municipal planning record, so their town readings cannot become `muni_zoning_rules` rows. They are kept in `assumptions/law/local_readings.yaml` and checked by `law/local_readings.py`:

- **Documents**: each text with its address, the date it was read, the SHA-256 of the file and of its text, its own dates as printed, `version_state` (final, superseded with `valid_until`, or draft) and `kind` (the bylaw, or the town's guidance, which is never the law).
- **Places**: one per town, city or county, with one fact per topic (districts that allow homes year round, duplex, small multi-unit buildings, the ADU rule, whether the local text still names an owner-occupancy condition, ADU size, parking minimums, what the bylaw says about water and sewer, the clause on unlisted uses, adoption). Each fact keeps its evidence: document, locator, offset or page, and a quote of at most 300 characters. A fact is `verified` only when every quote was found again at its locator in the saved copy and nothing is left to check; a fact that rests on reading table columns from the text layout, or on guidance, stays `draft` with its open check; a fact from a superseded text is `superseded`. A place that could not be read is `not_read`, with the reason.
- **Districts** (Vermont): what is known about each district in the terms of `rights.District`, so `district_inputs(place)` can be passed to `rights.state_floor`. Water service is known only where the bylaw itself says a district is served (Montpelier's purpose statements); elsewhere it stays unknown, and so does the four-unit right.
- **Dates**: a reading counts from its effective date, else from `in_force_by`, else from its review date (`counts_from`); the adoption date never stands in. `resident_coverage(state, population, total=..., on=...)` gives the share of a state's residents whose place has a verified reading in force on a date.

Read on 2026-11-26, from each town's own site: in Vermont Burlington, South Burlington, Montpelier, Winooski, Essex (town), Colchester, Milton, Hartford, Rutland city, Bennington and Barre city (11). Williston and St. Albans city were not read (their robots files refuse AI crawlers) nor Brattleboro (its bylaw is only on a file host whose download path is disallowed). Several bylaws still say an ADU needs an owner-occupied lot (South Burlington's, adopted August 3, 2026, Essex, Colchester, Milton, Barre, Bennington, and Burlington's review path), although Act 179 of 2026 removed that condition from the state's ADU protection from July 1, 2026; the readings report the local text and make no finding about conflict. In Wyoming: Laramie's development code (ADUs attached or detached in R1, LR and RR, one per lot, 220 to 1,200 sq ft, no owner-occupancy rule, no added parking; unlisted uses prohibited; no adoption or effective date printed, so it counts from its review date) and Teton County's former regulations (valid until July 1, 2026; superseded, so they say nothing about today's rule, which is only on a commercial code host). Using the Census Bureau's 2024 town population estimates, the verified Vermont readings cover 26.6% of the state's residents on 2026-11-26 and Laramie 5.6% of Wyoming's. On the runs' as-of date (2026-11-24) only the four Vermont bylaws that print an effective date count (Barre, Colchester, Essex, Montpelier: 7.0%), because the others count from their review date.

These readings never enter a benchmark headline: the Vermont missing-middle floor reads the statute (`statutory_rights.yaml`) and the pack's mapped sewer share, and Wyoming keeps the national defaults. They enter only through named variants (settings `local_readings` in `assumptions/variants.yaml`, code `engine/law_floor.py`): `vt_missing_middle_town_bylaws` (a town's verified district rights as the level already allowed where the bylaw allows more than the floor), `vt_parking_town_bylaws` and `wy_parking_local` (verified parking minimums into the parking mix). Only verified facts of readings in force on the run's as-of date count; drafts, guidance and superseded texts never do. They place a town on tracts by 2020 housing units through the pack's `tract_municipalities` (a 10-digit county subdivision `geoid`) and a Census place that is not a county subdivision (Wyoming's Laramie, 7-digit `geoid`) through `tract_places` (`law/places.py`: `place_pieces` gives the place pieces in the municipal table's shape, `readings_on_places` restates a place reading per county piece). ## Which ordinance to read next

`visionhousing law-priorities` ranks every unread **review unit** (a municipality with its own or joint zoning, or one county ordinance with all the municipalities it zones) and writes `data/outputs/law_priorities_<STATE>.csv` (not part of the repository). "Unread" uses the same in-force test as the hooks (`law/inforce.py`): a verified row with no known date, or one taking effect after the as-of date, stays on the list, and its note says so:

`score = unknown lots x sensitivity x representativeness / effort`

- **Unknown lots**: the unit's single-family lots whose rule is not verified.
- **Sensitivity**: the county's added ADU homes per single-family lot in the latest exported ADU benchmark run (`web/public/data/<STATE>/runs/adu.json`, P50), divided by the state's ratio; times a **switch factor**: in a county below the 50% bar, the share of the missing residents the unit would add (at least 0.1), because the engine uses a county's readings only once they pass the bar. Without the run, the ratio is 1 and the CSV says so.
- **Representativeness**: `1 + max(0, 0.5 - c) / 0.5`, where `c` is the share of lots read among municipalities of the same place type (the tract typology holding most of the unit's homes).
- **Effort**: 1 for an own-site PDF with a text layer, 3 for a scan, 5 when a text is posted but its operative part cannot be read yet (`own_text_incomplete`: Williamsport's use table is a placeholder page; Connellsville's text is undated and its 2022 update is a scan), 2 when nobody has looked; a unit whose only copy is on a commercial code host, or a county with no general zoning, scores 0 and says why. What is known about each unit is in `assumptions/law/review_sources.yaml`.

Parameters are in `assumptions/law/priorities.yaml`. The ranking is deterministic (ties by unit id). It orders the reading list; it changes no estimate.

## The rule-based reader (no AI)

`law/text.py` reads a PDF with pypdf (BSD licence), joins its pages, and cuts the text at every heading line (patterns in `assumptions/law/parser.yaml`), keeping each section's start and end offsets. `law/extract.py` ranks sections by keyword counts per topic and pulls values with fixed patterns: the ADU status phrase nearest the first ADU keyword (trying up to five sections, because the best match is often the definitions), "minimum lot area ... N square feet" in residential sections, "N spaces per dwelling unit" lines split into single-family and multi-family, housing-type words, and "transportation impact fee". Every value keeps a quote of at most 240 characters, its offset and its section. `draft_rule` makes a `draft` row with `method: rule`; the schema refuses a verified row that is not a hand reading, so the reader can never verify anything.

```
visionhousing law-draft --pdf ordinance.pdf --url https://borough.example/zoning.pdf --geoid 4213370568 --out outputs/law/review.csv
```

A person then checks each line of the review CSV against the PDF and copies what holds into `municipal_rules.yaml` as a verified row.

How well it reads real ordinances: on the six municipal ordinances read by hand, its ADU status agreed with the hand reading for three (Derry: special exception; Waynesboro and Wyomissing: not addressed), left three unknown (Pittsburgh, Shrewsbury, Hermitage: their rule sits in tables or struck text) and got none wrong. Lot and parking values in tables are often missed or wrong. That is why its rows stay drafts.

## What feeds the engine

`law/hooks.py` works out, per county, an already-legal ADU share weighted by residents (read municipalities by their rule, unread ones at the statewide range; a verified row whose `adu_status` is blank or `unknown` does not answer the ADU question, so for this hook it counts as unread, in `b`, never as "not legal"; `adu_status_unknown: treat_as_unread` in `hooks.yaml`), the share of read residents under a parking minimum above zero, and the share under a transportation impact fee ordinance. A county uses its own value only when verified rows cover at least `min_coverage` (50%) of its residents (`assumptions/law/hooks.yaml`). Every other county keeps the statewide range.

The hooks are switched on (`engine_wired: true`) for the ADU share and parking; the fee hook stays off (`wired.fees: false`), because no reading answers the impact-fee question yet.

- **ADU share.** For a qualifying county, `share = a + b x s`, where `a` is the share of its residents who live where ADUs are allowed by right in a verified reading, `b` the share whose rules are not read, and `s` each Monte Carlo draw of the statewide `adu.already_legal_share`. So the statewide knob still moves the county, the range stays a range, and the county's tracts get `a + b x s` instead of `s` in the ADU funnel's first step. Only `by_right` counts as legal; `not_addressed` does not. Warren County's ordinance allows a second house on lots of 1.5 acres or more; that is a second principal dwelling, not an ADU rule, so its 14 municipalities stay `not_addressed`.
- **Parking reach.** For a qualifying county, the parking estimate is scaled by `1 - residents read with a parking minimum of zero / residents`. Unread residents, and read ones with no parking answer, keep the statewide assumption that a minimum applies.
- **Where it shows.** The ledger entry `adu.already_legal_share` (Assumption Ledger, `GET /ledger?state=PA`) says which counties use read local law and cites the readings. Each such county's ADU and parking numbers carry the citations in their formula and the source `pa_municipal_ordinances`. The county panel ("Local rules read") and the place profile's zoning summary say it in plain words.

Until 2026-11-25 three counties qualified (Warren, Fayette and Pike). Since "not addressed" no longer counts as an answer without a clause on unlisted uses, **no county qualifies for the ADU hook**: Fayette's 31 and Warren's 14 county-zoned municipalities are unknown (their ordinances' clauses on unlisted uses were not read), which drops Fayette from 64% to 0% and Warren from 71% to 24% of residents with an ADU answer, and Pike from 50% to below the bar. Their ADU shares go back to the statewide range. The parking hook counts any read rule, so the same three counties still qualify for it, with unchanged factors (every read municipality there has a parking minimum above zero). The lot-weighted categories changed in 12 counties (Allegheny, Berks, Bucks, Dauphin, Elk, Fayette, Franklin, Luzerne, Pike, Schuylkill, Warren, York). In Allegheny, Pittsburgh's 18.9% of lots is already permitted, as before; the not-addressed suburbs move from prohibited (20.1% of lots) to discretionary (7.8%, up from 3.4%), prohibited (9.0%) and unknown. The numbers are in the engine's output, not here.

## Profile

- `zoning`: for a county, the share of residents whose municipality has been read and verified (compared with the state), the municipalities under county zoning, and each verified municipality's ADU rule; for a tract (or a one-municipality county), the ADU rule with citation, date, method and review status, the smallest residential lot, the parking minimums and the housing types.
- `plans`: the county plan (all 67 counties) and the planning commission's link.
- `state_law` (new): the MPC sections per reform (saying when they do not reach Philadelphia or Pittsburgh) and each bill's status, last action and the share of residents (a tract: 2020 homes) it would reach; for ADU bills, a place whose verified rule is already by right is said to change little.
  For a state without the `state_law_provisions` table (Massachusetts, Vermont, Wyoming, any new state), the section reads `assumptions/law/states/<st>.yaml`, the state's records in `assumptions/law/timeline.yaml` and `assumptions/calibration/already_legal_<st>.yaml` (`visionhousing.law.states`). One fact per provision gives its status, effective date, citation, check date and whether it is in force on the run's as-of date (`timeline.eligibility_status` on its dated records; no dated record says "not known"). The already-legal ranges are shown with their low and high ends. A state whose provisions have no dated record says "No state reform law found (checked <date>)" (Wyoming, re-checked 2026-11-26 against every bill and enacted act of 2024 to 2026). Wyoming's 2026 Fast Track Permits Act (HB0002, W.S. 16-13-101 and 16-13-102, effective July 1, 2026: a 30-day decision clock on residential permits for one or two homes) is kept in the state file under `non_benchmark_enacted`, not under `provisions`, because it changes none of the five reforms; the file also lists the bills read and not enacted (`bills_not_enacted`), among them 2025 HB0197 (a cap on housing exaction fees) and 2025 SF0040 (postponed indefinitely). In Massachusetts, a tract names every Section 3A district whose outline reaches it, with the share of the district inside the tract (pack table `mbta_3a_district_tracts`; a pack built before that table falls back to the district's centre tract, `mbta_3a_districts.tract`); a county counts its districts and towns. A provision is shown under its own `source_id` or its records' (Vermont's Acts 152 and 179 records name `vt_acts_2026`); without one, under the registry entry whose address is closest to its own. The fact note lists the record's own addresses. The check date shown is the later of the provision's `checked_on` (its last re-reading) and its record's `verified_on`.

## Limits

- 174 municipalities out of about 2,570 are read and verified (112 of them through five county ordinances: Fayette, Warren, Schuylkill, Luzerne and Lycoming), as `visionhousing law-tables` reports; everything else is unknown. Posted copies of ordinances can lag amendments. Most municipalities publish their zoning only on a commercial code host, which this project does not read; `assumptions/law/review_sources.yaml` lists where that was found, and the priority list scores those units zero.
- A county ordinance that does not mention ADUs is read as `not_addressed`; without a read clause on unlisted uses it is unknown, not "not already legal". Warren County allows a second house on lots of 1.5 acres or more; that is not counted as an ADU rule.
- The Vermont rights test needs facts the pack does not have yet for most districts (water and sewer service, the unit requirement); until a district's service is known its four-unit right is reported as unknown. The missing-middle funnel turns that unknown into a likely range with two draft judgement entries (the share of sewered homes also on municipal water, and the share of homes with no sewer record served by both), because the pack has a mapped sewer layer but no statewide water layer.
- Summaries are ours, not legal advice; the texts are the authority. The MPC text on palegis.us is unofficial.
- A bill's reach counts municipalities by the DCED zoning record; its own exceptions (deed restrictions, local options) are not modelled. HB 1459's reach is not worked out.
- The reader cannot read pictures of tables, struck-through text or scanned pages. A page the PDF reader fails on (a damaged page) is left empty and listed in `DocText.unreadable_pages`; `law-draft` prints those page numbers so a person reads them by eye.
- Open States bulk bill data needs an account, so it is not used; bill status comes from each bill's page.

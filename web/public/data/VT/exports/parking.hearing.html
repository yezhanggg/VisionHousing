Hearing brief: Parking requirement reduction, Vermont

Decision support, not a forecast. Not an official state document.

The question

Over 2027–2031, how many more homes would Vermont likely authorize by building permit with parking requirement reduction, compared with today's rules?

The answer, as a likely range

Over 2027–2031, Vermont would likely permit between 34 and 170 more homes (0.43–1.2%) with parking minimums removed, on top of a baseline of 5,100–18,000 homes expected under today's rules.

What the headline counts

  • Parking requirement reduction: the headline counts comparable. It is the Census-comparable figure.

Main drivers

  • Extra units a small site can hold when parking is not required: about 45 homes at its low value (0%), about 150 at its high value (15%).
  • Baseline housing cycle (permits at the low or high end of their likely range): about 33 homes with permits at the low end of their range, about 110 at the high end.
  • Medium-run supply response to a change in profit per unit: about 48 homes at its low value (×0.3), about 120 at its high value (×2).

What is unknown

  • Baseline housing cycle (permits at the low or high end of their likely range): 46% of the uncertainty.
  • Extra units a small site can hold when parking is not required: 14% of the uncertainty.
  • Medium-run supply response to a change in profit per unit: 9% of the uncertainty.
  • None: this reform creates no home by addition or conversion.
  • Unavailable: no completion series is read for this state, so no observed completion is reported.
  • Not modelled: no completion lag is applied, so no permit is turned into a delivered home here. The authorized net change if completed is the upper limit of what these permits could add to the stock.
  • No mechanism in this reform pushes a permit past 2031; a start delay lowers the total instead (permits not issued are not moved).
  • The survey covers places that issue building permits; a place with no permit requirement is outside it. The Census Bureau imputes figures for permit offices that did not report, and since 2023 it updates the set of permit offices every year. The baseline projects the published figures, imputations included.
  • Local rules not yet read (unresolved): 100% of residents; the engine uses its statewide fallback range there.

What the model cannot tell you

  • Whether lenders in a given market would finance buildings with fewer spaces.
  • Effects on street parking and on neighbours.
  • Whether land freed from parking becomes homes or something else.
  • The baseline repeats recent permit trends under today's rules. It does not predict a recession or a boom.
  • These are ranges of building permits, not a forecast of homes finished.
  • A reform's likely range is a simulation range: it shows how the result moves with the assumptions, given the baseline. No reform of this kind has past totals to check it against.

Housing reforms in Pennsylvania: homes permitted, 2027–2031

Decision support, not a forecast. Not an official state document.

In one sentence

Over 2027–2031, Pennsylvania would likely permit between 95 and 1,500 more homes (0.069–1.3%) with two to six homes allowed on single-family lots, on top of a baseline of 67,000–180,000 homes expected under today's rules.

What moves this estimate most

Each input below is moved alone from its low to its high value while every other input stays at its central value. The figures are the five-year homes this reform adds at each end.

  1. Share of feasible lots redeveloped each year: about 76 homes at its low value (0.05% a year), about 1,500 at its high value (1% a year).
  2. Capitalisation rate used to value the finished building: about 1,100 homes at its low value (5% a year), about 110 at its high value (7.5% a year).
  3. Construction cost per square foot, 2-6 unit building: about 1,100 homes at its low value ($150/sq ft), about 120 at its high value ($275/sq ft).
  4. Rent premium of a new 2-6 unit building's homes over the census two-bedroom median: about 160 homes at its low value (×1.05), about 1,100 at its high value (×1.5).
  5. Floor area per missing-middle unit: about 1,100 homes at its low value (750 sq ft), about 160 at its high value (1,300 sq ft).

What this cannot tell you

  • For-sale townhouses and condominiums: every new building is valued as a rental.
  • Which lots sit side by side, or which owners would sell.
  • The real price of a house bought to be torn down; census home values are owners' own estimates.
  • Local zoning detail: the share of lots zoned for one home only is a statewide range.
  • Lending and building-code rules for the larger buildings in this range.

What these estimates cannot tell you

  • These are likely ranges of building permits, not a forecast of homes finished.
  • They describe the whole state. They do not say which lots, streets or owners would build.
  • They do not say who would live in the new homes or what they would pay.
  • They assume every town follows the new rule from the first day and approves permits at today's pace.
  • They say nothing about years after the five-year period.

The baseline

  • The baseline repeats recent permit trends under today's rules. It does not predict a recession or a boom.
  • Counties without a permit office, or with imputed permit counts, carry the Census Bureau's estimates.

How the ranges are made

Every uncertain assumption has a low, central and high value in the Assumption Ledger. The model runs 2,000 times (seed 0), drawing each assumption from its range, and reports the spread of answers. Run it again with the same seed and every number comes out the same.

How the headline numbers are worked out

  • baseline permitted units 2027-2031, state: P50 = sum of county P50s = 141,660 units; range from the same rule (mean of 2021-2025 permits) backtested on state totals at 21 past origins (state point projection 151,882 units): P10 = P50 × 0.488 / 1.038, P90 = P50 × 1.314 / 1.038 (ratio P10, typical cycle q0.5, P90) = P10 66,560 units, P50 141,660 units, P90 179,300 units
  • added units, Missing middle 2027-2031, state: P10, P50, P90 of 2,000 seeded draws of the sum over counties of added units per tract, summed to the county in each draw; per draw: the sum over tracts of net added units = net units x (1 - displacement share x) = 94.59 units, 439.62 units, 1,484 units
  • combined added units, Missing middle 2027-2031, state: P10, P50, P90 of 2,000 seeded draws of sum of individual effects - overlap removed + interaction added, in each draw = 94.59 units, 439.62 units, 1,484 units

Technical notes from the model

  • Setbacks and lot coverage are assumed not to bind, as the Handbook allows.
  • Lot-size fit from: lot_proxy.
  • Each draw places the baseline at one position in its likely range, the same for every county; reforms acting on baseline production use the same position.
  • A lot is used once: when ADU and missing middle are both on, the lots the ADU reform builds on leave the missing-middle pool (missing_middle.adu_lot_overlap_share).
  • When the fee cut is on, the ADU and 2-6 unit pro formas' fee lines are cut by the same share; when parking reform is on, their parking lines are scaled by the share of spaces still built outside transit-rich tracts.
  • Fee and parking savings on baseline production are added; their cross term is not modelled.

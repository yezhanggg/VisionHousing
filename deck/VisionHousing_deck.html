VisionHousing

If the state changed the rules, how many more homes would be permitted?

An open-source tool that gives a likely range for the extra homes a state housing reform would bring over five years, shows what drives it, and says what it cannot tell you.

Every number traceable. No AI inside the tool. Apache-2.0.

The problem

State analysts must answer a hard question quickly

The question

  • A bill would allow backyard homes, small apartment buildings or fewer parking minimums. How many more homes would that bring, and where?
  • Answers today are single numbers with no reasons, or studies of one city.

The user

  • A state policy analyst or legislative staffer, not a modeller.
  • Needs a defensible range, the main reasons, and the limits, on one screen.
  • Needs to show the work to colleagues who will question it.

What the tool does

It answers two questions

1. What if the state changed the rules?

Five benchmark reforms, alone or together. A likely range of the extra homes permitted over five years, against a no-change baseline, by state, county and district.

2. What is this place like today?

For any county or census tract: people, homes and prices, jobs, what is being built, zoning, water and sewer, flood risk. One printable page.

The outcome

We count homes authorized by building permits over five years

2027–2031

the five-year window

  • Outcome: housing units authorized by building permits, summed over five years.
  • Source: the Census Bureau's Building Permits Survey, every county, every year.

Why permits

  • Published for every county in every state, the same way, with years of history.
  • Out within months, so the baseline can be checked against what happened.
  • A permit is the step a reform changes first. Not every permit becomes a home; we say so.

Permit files used: bps_county_annual: 1990-2025 annual files (the 2025 file is the latest published)

Method

Four steps, each one simple enough to check

  1. Baseline, back-tested. Carry forward recent permits by county. The rule won a contest on past years, judged by a test fixed in advance.
  2. A funnel per reform. Lots allowed, lots that fit, projects that pay, owners who build. Each step is a number you can open.
  3. Paired Monte Carlo. 2,000 simulated futures. Baseline and reform share each future, so the reform's effect is measured like for like.
  4. Ranges. We report P10 to P90: eight in ten simulated futures fall inside. Never one number.

Real bills

The same engine scores bills now before the legislature

  • Pennsylvania HB 2186: one ADU by right on any residential lot
  • Pennsylvania SB 1346: ADUs on single-family detached lots outside Philadelphia and Pittsburgh

A pending bill never changes the baseline; it is scored only in its own run.

Budget-capped ADU financing ($100,000,000, $40,000 per award): the program alone adds 211 to 1,200; with the ADU law change, 4,400 to 16,400. The original financing variant has no appropriation limit: every owner who wants help gets it. Its paired change is a scenario difference under that assumption, never the result of a budget-capped program.

Reforms together

Reforms do not simply add up

  • A lot is counted once. When two reforms could use the same lot, the overlap is taken out.
  • Savings from one reform that feed another are added; the small cross effect between the fee cut and parking reform is left out.

Limits

What these estimates cannot tell you

  • These are ranges of building permits, not a forecast of homes finished.
  • A reform's likely range is a simulation range: it shows how the result moves with the assumptions, given the baseline. No reform of this kind has past totals to check it against.
  • They describe the whole state. They do not say which lots, streets or owners would build.
  • They do not say who would live in the new homes or what they would pay.
  • They assume every town follows the new rule from the first day and approves permits at today's pace.
  • They say nothing about years after the five-year period.

What's next

Where the tool goes from here
